# Phone prices are climbing everywhere

[![](https://cdn.example.com/w_1200/phones.jpg)](https://cdn.example.com/full/phones.jpg)

Phones on sale in Shenzhen. Photo by [Lin Wei](https://commons.example.org/wiki/File:Phones.jpg)

Memory chips have doubled in price this year, and the cheapest phones feel it first. Makers that sell mostly entry-level devices have cut whole product lines rather than raise prices on buyers who cannot pay more.

The increases vary sharply by region: prices rose by a fifth in South Asia and the Middle East, but only slightly in the United States, where premium phones dominate sales.

<https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/AbC12/3/>

## Why memory matters

A phone's memory is one of its few parts with no cheaper substitute, so when its price rises the whole bill of materials rises with it. Analysts expect the squeeze to last at least another year.

[![](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/dQw4w9WgXcQ/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ)

Manufacturers explained their choices in a recorded briefing, and several said they would rather ship fewer phones than ship worse ones.

[![](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/IfNG7wZSxtg/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IfNG7wZSxtg)
