Sleeper services are back, and they are full

Ten years ago the night train looked finished. Budget airlines had undercut it, rail operators had stopped replacing the rolling stock, and one by one the famous routes went dark.

Now the sleepers are back. New lines link Vienna, Paris, Brussels and Berlin, and on summer weekends the couchettes sell out weeks in advance, as travellers trade an hour of flying for a night of rocking sleep.

A six-berth couchette, made up for the night.

Operators say the economics have changed. Carbon-conscious travellers will pay a premium, and the new carriages fit more passengers into the same length of train. Still, the margins are thin.
